## New findings (2026-10-13, from the physics tests)
- **Time step is the dominant error.** Implicit Euler is first order. At the production
  setting of 60 steps/period, the amplitude ratio is ~8% above its dt → 0 limit and the
  phase ~1.3° below it (q=10, 30 Hz, Γ=0.2: 0.998 at 60 steps vs. ~0.92 extrapolated).
  Options: run at 240+ steps/period (the LU cache grows with it, so use the GMRES path), or
  switch to a second-order scheme (Crank–Nicolson/BDF2). Re-check the experiment
  comparison either way.
- **Pressure quadrature is first order.** It integrates a uniform pressure over the disk
  to πR²(1 + 1/(2q) + 1/(12q²)) instead of πR², about 1% at q=50. MATLAB has the same.
- **`D25Quant200` cache is incomplete:** rows 564–2500 are zero. It is now rejected, not
  registered. The production `D50Quant100` cache is verified on spot rows.

## LU cache vs preconditioned GMRES (n = 5002; measured on 4 vCPU, 2 BLAS threads)
lu 4.34 s · ldiv 17.5 ms · matvec 13.4 ms · pGMRES 1–5 iterations, flat across nr = 100–300.
Real cases run 660–1320 steps.

| Path | Cost per case | Memory |
|---|---|---|
| LU cache, 60 factors | 260 s + 12–23 s = **272–283 s** | 12 GB |
| LU cache, deduped to 31 factors | **146–158 s** | 6.2 GB |
| pGMRES, 1 factor, 5 iterations (worst case) | 4 s + 111–222 s = **115–226 s** | 0.2 GB (+ small Krylov basis) |
| plain GMRES (current fallback) | ≥ 5002 × 13.4 ms ≈ **67 s per step** | 0.4 GB |

Conclusion: on time alone, preconditioned GMRES is break-even with a deduped LU cache.
It wins on memory and, by a wide margin, against the current GMRES fallback. It only
speeds up sweeps when memory, not cores, limits how many cases run at once. Unverified:
the iteration count at nr = 2500.
